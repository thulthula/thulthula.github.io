#The Current approach and scaling:
The Base Architecture of current AI LLMs:
the current LLMs are basically a large set of numbers, one can imagine it as a excel file filled with numbers, billions and billions (or even trillion in some cases) of numbers, thats it, as joked by elon musk that our digital god is just a csv file?. 

Those numbers are called the 'weights' of the model. Whenever we hear that model is being trained or AI is being trained, we are just talking about the process of acquiring these numbers. Now how can be these numbers be interpreted as the info in the real world?
Lets understand this with the help of an example: Lets say our model essentially indentifies a given image as dog or a cat. This model (large excel of numbers) can be thought of as a big function which is applied over that image? How? An image is basiclly a set of numbers for a computer, each pixel has its pixel values which tells the intensity of the led and they are arranged in a grid. 

So this function(f) basically maps those image 'numbers' to either zero for cat or one for dog:

F([12,213,23,123],...)

Now how is it able to do that? The answer lies in the training or learning of those numbers. In the training process this model is presented with thousands of cat and dog images, and trained with a algorithm called as backpropogation. This feild of ai is filled with these bizz words. It is a simple idea, all those weights (which were initailed with random numbers) were changed one by one so that the function outputs the label dog when presented with a dog image and vice versa for a cat image. Now this classifier can be easily made with around 10k parameters, but imagine changing those billions and billions of numbers one by one so that those LLMs become accurate, and this is the reasons it costs million or even billions of dollars to train these models.

I would like to point to statement which we will discuss later that: A human child didn't need those 10k images before able to confidently identify a dog or a cat? 

Ok but how these LLMs work?. To do so firstly these words needs to be converted into numbers or vectors. To do so, firstly a vocabulary is defined, that is LLMs know only these words/parts of words like aa,aaaa, ion, tion, etc. For a GPT-2 model it was around 54,000 tokens. These tokens are arranged in an alphabetical order and then each token is assigned a vector (which is again learned, it is called tokenization process). After the words are converted to tokens then a huge task of next token prediction happens over the entire dataset of internet, where essentially the model is expected to predict the next word of the sentence, if it predictes then it is rewarded, if it predicts wrong token then it is punished. The reward is done by calculaing a score, called loss, which captures the info that how wrong the model is, and the complete objective of the training process is to reduce this loss. 

How was the researches able to make such a function? they didn't: all they did was create a architecture consisting of those billion of paramters which can be trained. That famous architecture is called Attention: introduced by Google in paper: Attention is all you need. This eye catching paper basiclly defies a way so that the loss is calculated and those parameters is arranged so that model pays 'attention' to previous related tokens, How? Again a recurring theme: it is learned from the data itself, we tend to repeat words which we have paid attention earlier, like for example i should not have mentioned the word sky-diving, or ice-cream or piano in this essay abruptly(although writing the words "i should not have mentioed the words" beat the purpose), as humans whatever we write, we pay attention to previous words and tokens and that essentially is piced up by these LLM architectures. 

Second point i would draw attention is: Once those numbers(weights) are fixed, they are frozen.

Are we even headed in the right direction?
Claiming that we are not even headed in the right direction when AI is able to beat in so many wide variet of tasks is a bold statement. 

But it comes down to how we measure progress? We measure progress with benchmarks such as MATH-500, HumanityLastExam etc. with is similar to what we humans do, for example clearing a test or JEE Advanced. But unline humans these benchmarks do not measure intelligence (do note that not all human benchmarks measure intelligence). 

The defination of intelligence is
> The ability to acquire and apply knowledge

The ability of applying knowledge is totally covered by those benchmarks, but the ability to acquire that knowledge, is no where tested. There is no emphasis on 'learning'

A AI agent should learn from anything, an agent should have goals, autonomously control its data stream and teach itself based on those goals. 

It should not not require million and million of examples to learn a single task, as this will only restrict leaning where million and million of examples exist, but most hard tasks do not have so much data, they are very niche and require learning from small examples.

An Agent architecture should be such that it can be taught just as done to a human, a back and forth interaction rather thatn learning from a data corpse. An it should be continuously learn from its current data without steering or feedback.

We are at weird spot with AI where it can ace phd level exams, discover new drugs, yet it cant even learn simplest of process without million and millions of data.

The inspiration and much focus of the feild should be on making better and better model architecture and reinforcement learning.

Some early efforts were seen all the back in 1930s in RL with real focus on leaning with Thomas Ross' Thinking Machines and Steven Smith's Robotic Rat experiment which leaened to navigate mazes. 

Early AI was mostly about animal like continual learning systems and it was well acknowledged that intelligence is fundamentally about the ability to learn. To say that modern AI does not learn is wrong, but instead of having differnt phases like Traning, Fine Tuning, Human feedback learning, Testing, Deployemnt, and after which the weights never change again, instead there should be a single phase where the model is both learning and doing. 
While the modern AI has gotten more general like one agent can do many tasks from things like Making Recepie and writing code, with the popular direction of research we will never have an AI that can LEARN any tasks. 
Instead we should focus on general purpose learning algorithm inspired by animal learning(which ofcourse include humans), brain strucutre and previous psycology research about how a new information is gathered, precessed and stored in out memory.

Ideas on How the architecture should be made:

We started with mimicking a single neuron and created a perceptron: 
In neuron, dendrites receive signals from neighbournig neurons and adds them up in the cll body, If the compined signals is greater than a certain threshold (determiend by neuron's membrane potential) then neuron fires and sends a electrical impulse through axon to the next neuron. Similarly a perceptron, a node takes in multiple inputs, combines them using weights and bias and passes the result though an activation function (such as ReLU, which only passes the positive inputs). If the activation condition is met the output is passed forward to next layer.

The current LLM architecture rely mostly around multiple Attention and MLP(multi layered perception) Blocks. These blocks are repeated hundreads of times in big LLMs, which constitudes most of the weights and last layer has functions which converts back those activations(vectors) to human interpretable words.

But human brain is not this straightforward, and we should try to take inspiration while making these LLM architectures. For example lets discuss one such change case study (inspired from book: Thinking Fast and Slow, Daniel Kahnmann)

The human thinking can be broken down into interaction between two characters or systems. System 1 and System 2: System 1 is responsible for automatic, and quick thinking, it takes little or no effort and we have no sense of voluntart control over it, it happenes in background all the time. whereas system2 allocates attention to memory activity according to the demand of the task. We have a choice and control over it, like for example how effortful we want to be, how much effort is required for the taks. 
System 1 work can be such as detecting if one object is clise or far away from one, answer of 2+2 = ?, or reading words from a large billboard, finding strong move in chess or understanding simple sentences. Mental Activity become part of System 1 and it access memory without intention or effort. System 2 reuire attention and is disrupted when attention is drawn away, its work examples can be focusing on a voice in a crowded environment, searching memory for a particular sound, checking tha validity of a complex logic.

System 1 and System 2 go hand in hand, the control of attention is shared between system 1 and 2 (for example attention directed to a comment(managed by s1), and then framing a response to it(managed by s2)). System 2 has the ability to change the way of working of s1 working termorairly or permanently (for example to set yourself to look for white shirt person in crowd). Attention has limited budget and is expensive, going beyond the budget cause thighs to fail. Intense focus make people go blins to stimuli that attact attention, therefore s1 still needs some attention for its funstioning. Other parameters are also controlled, such as Most of s2 thiking (seed) originates in s1 itself and s2 takes control when things get diffucult, s2 also activates when an event voilates the model of the world s1 maintaines and it increases effort when it detects an error is about ot be made, s2 always has the last work in an conflict.
